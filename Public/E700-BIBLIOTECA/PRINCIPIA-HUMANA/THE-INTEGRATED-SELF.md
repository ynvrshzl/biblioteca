# Title
The complex "I" and the integrated "I"

## Context
Carl Jung developed psychology concepts on the self and integration. 

## Integration
Why is integration such a powerful, significant...? Integration is unification, healing and order. In a world where the psyche is increasingly disturbed and fragmented; integration is an oasis; A force of life to be reckoned with.

In computer-science, abstraction is the tech-equivalent of integration. It is a powerful function, perhaps one of the most powerful.

## Self
The psyche is hungry. We come to the world starving.

## Phenomenological

From local, phenomenological experience; The self wants to be seen. The self needs to be seen, to be listened, to be witnessed. The self needs to know it is real. That it's pain is real. That it's joy is real. That what we believe is real; that the beliefs we hold are not merely fragments of neuro-chemical concoction. That life is not futile, simply becuase we die. 

All of this is made whole when the self is seen. To reveal your true form, is to be yourself.

I feel this is something of like an interwoven thread of the fabric of the complex I; "the integrated I."

The psyche is fragmented, by trauma, by love, by betrayal, by joy. By experience, by life. 

The task of the psynaut, therefore, is to bring order and healing, integration and restoration, to make what is broken whole; for healing is bringing broken parts together (wabi-sabi.)

## Math as a language
At the base of mathematics, before arithmetic, before operations and even before axioms, the bedrock consists of rules and observations about the relationships, interactions and truths of quantitative transformation; in other words, rules to describe how "x" changed, is allowed to change, etc.

These rules imply an immaterial aspect; neither discovery nor invention. Meaning is not arbitrary; and logic is not self-defining; everything comes from something else, nothing is created from nothing. Math is not a substance of the aether, and it is not a proof of simulation theory; it follows the natural laws of semiotics and physics; symbols themselves are meaningless until an author establishes a definition. Language transforms symbol, by defining it's meaning, based on ideas, observations or information, and such an abstract symbol is associated with it's meaning upon retrieval.

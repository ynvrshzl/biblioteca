# Field of actionable phenomenology
What etymological components, words, terms or parts may describe a field, whom's central thesis revolves around the heart of phenomenology, not as philoosphy, but as an actionable field... "we are sharing the same physical space, but we are not experiencing it as a shared similarity." Now, this appears as a philosphical thought at face value; but beneath the facade, this is perhaps the most important subset of psychology; different individuals respond to the same situation. Almost as if we each contain an internal climate, reactive to different sets of phenomena. For example, one person may be deeply drawn to logistics, while another person may see the world through an extraverted lens. 

Psychology contains categorization of these personality traits infact, "the big five." However, this field is not studying the umbrella; we are focusing on the first-person perspective psychology. Before empirical classifcations, we begin with the phenomenological: "why do I feel disconnected from my words and the people of the room I am in?" 

That is the basis of the field... a bridge between what we experience as human beings, what is recorded, observable, solveable, solved and transformed into real change. That relationship is the case study!

The central thesis "we experience the same space, differently" is the segue into one of the most implementable concepts that I have seen verifiable effects of; something like "psychohabitats." This helps explain why different parts of a person's personality may seclude into introversion, when in specific contexts.

The implementable aspect of "habitats of the psyche" is understanding the ecological relationship between the physical enviornment, physical self, the physiological, and the eventual autonomous human decision-making which shapes the enviornment... as a form of cybernetic relationship.

Understanding how the enviornment shapes, influences and changes our behavior, is one half of the equation; the transformation of change occurs when a person modifies their physical environment, such that it habituates positive effects on the physiological.

This enviornment is not only external; but internal aswell. (...we may explore this eventually...)

A clinical term for this field "psychoecology" exists as "ecopsychology" which is not essentially far from the heart of this field. Perhaps psychoecology may be a proper term, but it is temporary for the time being. As it most likely does not capture the phenomenological heart of the efforts...!

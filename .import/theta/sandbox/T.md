
In history, how did various thinkers, figures or traditions and civilizations, deal with the problem of having unpredictable days? 



Anthropologically, it is understood that our human ancestors worked in cyclical, organic patterns. Apparently, time was also not fixed by a number; it was embodied. Sleep wasn't disturbed via alarm, it was synchronized with the outside world. 



Today however, we are often forced or conditioned to be as productive as possible becuase "life is short" and "time is money."



This is a central problem in my local practice... I believe "Principia Economica" can become a survey into the real history of human work, health and social integration. So we have access to real information; what really drives the human machinery; not idealized industrial clockwork.



Aspects of time, scheduling, and various synthetic/artificial disturbances in modern life, are not the most catastrophic aspect of this problem; we human beings can adapt and survive in incredibly diverse ecosystems. However, the social narrative behind work, and identity, and value... is the problem i'm focusing on; it isn't a person's fault if they cannot focus or be productive or make money; there is real science to diagnose why this happens and what we can realistically do.



This is essentially my developing work of "Principia Economica: The 21st century." a unifying study, at the intersection of

- Natural human work cycles

- Sustainable effort & exchange of energy within systems

- Issues and Benefits of modern industrialization



Somedays will be amazing across all metrics; productive, healthy, conscientious, integrated, orderly, meaningful, progressive. And other days go by as if they never happened; little to no memory, exhausting. As if some days are merely survival, and other days are "blue-moon" phenomena, so-to-speak. Where for some reason, areas in the human life align and harmonize. In this sense, there are branches of integral study;

- Paradox of Productivity: Productivity is not a product, it is a byproduct (which is a paradox!)

- Areas of harmony; what are these areas that seem to emerge naturally, and rarely? How did our ancestors live in according to these interwoven rules and properties of living systems?

- Human life cannot merely be about utility, achievement, or the pursuit of destination. In this case, Aristotle's "Telos" is a powerful concept, but not sufficient for the local-scale; what must one do when they cannot actively progress towards a direction of meaning? That is the mystery zone we explore in this work.

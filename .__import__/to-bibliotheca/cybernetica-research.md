# Phenomenological spaces in Computation and Organics
For the longest time, during my phenomenological research with computer systems and complex management, I suspected the physical boundaries of the machine were to blame: a computational device today is closer to a modern automobile, than the original ford conception of an engine powering wheels (important). Today, machines have integrated several non-related systems, just as the vehicle industry has increasingly integrated several hierarchies of human needs. Spanning from the most essential, the engine, reliability, spanning to conventions (windshield, doors, ) conviences (cooling and heating systems, sound systems, etc.) and accessibility (wheel-chair support, emergency transportation, etc.) This similar phenomenon is replicated in the architecture of modern devices. Today, a mobile phone has access to hundreds of hidden technologies; and they all share the same surface area. It is a miracle of coordinated, collective human engineering and communication, but it also presented a problem; is it healthy for our nervous system to have an office, a calculator, a phone, a camera, a gps, an internet browser, an entertainment factory, communication, access to news, (etc...) in the exact same surface area?

This is what's fascinating; when we observe the natural world, this phenomenon is known as Continuity (... i think...). The same park can be the enviornment for a multitude of different events ; in this case, the same physical space exhibits multi-purposes, matching context. That is most likely why parks, cities, and complex enviornments are widespread; a reflection of modern life; not merely convenience, but perhaps recycling.

The same idea applies to a human being too; and at even more complex levels of analysis. The same person can exhibit different sides of the self (not personalities, important distinction.) The same person is also evolving; the same body houses complex memories, experiences and windows of phenomenology.

So if a shared-space can sustain diverse settings, and the same applies for a person... we may see a reflection of this in our own engineering... and in some sense, engineering is reverse-engineering nature... (we can branch this into another study.)

Something like.. "context-driven or multi-context meta-engineering" where the context influences the function of form... e.g., a party at the mall, a birthday, even a wedding, can all share similar spaces, yet contain entirely different ontologies.

This also introduces a significant engineering problem; how can a physical form contain functions not present as affordances? In other words... The computer fits similar ontology; For an everyday computer, hardware must be stable, and software should ideally be dynamic (self-updating, error-correcting, human-authored, secure, etc.)...

This study, weaves into an eventual cohesive bedrock, for the study of technology and human life; viewed from both the system and the experienced, the phenomenolgical and ontological levels of analysis. One of these projects includes Cybernetica: a series of research for cognitive ergonomic operating systems, designed by the human being; not a vendor.

Before any technological implementation whatsoever; we have to understand the system and how it affects us, becuase the irresonspible, epistemic-laden development of engineering, for the sake of construction, is where we have arrived at today; devices, machines and systems where convenience and accessibility are interwoven, where they should not be. 

I argue the utility of form is defined by it's function (meaning as use) and the justification of it's development, is relative to the scale of problems it solves. This is the basis of meta-engineering (if it's not a field, we should develop it further, branching from the heart of this study.)

This is where HCI is significant. But this study is still in it's early conception stages. The heart of the arguments is captured in the symbiotic relationship between how we shape our tools, and how they shape us. (citation unavailable, this quote is well-known, but mis-attributed.)

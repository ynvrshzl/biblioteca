We recently had a discussion about what i consider a fascinating state of human being... soemthing like "when the journey towards a valued destination, justifies the conditions."

Now I want to emphasize certain things, such as evil, cannot and should not be justified. However, from a nihilstic sense, I do believe to exist, requires existential justficiation, and that requires confrontation with the unconscious, in a Jungian sense.

When the journey itself... the struggle... the challenges, and sometimes even the moments where giving up is genuinely tempting... to overcome all of that and persist? That's something beyond just an organic drive to survive... that's like a force of life, captured almost at full resolution in the mythic. I truly believe Jung was seeing closer to reality at native resolution... becuase i've witnessed personal experiences eerily similar to his work.

But this discussion isn't meant to focus on the dark... I am focusing on this genuinely special beautiful mode of being... and it's something that has to be constantly recalibrated... so it's more of like a flame... it's definitely something like the will to live! I think this is what drives good parents to sacrifice for their family... or a doctor to perform surgery for hours on end... when sacrifice, or the exhaustion of becoming, is justified... I think that's something like a natural source of energy for the human being... and the MAIN emphasis of this messy set of observations, is that THIS may be one of the fundamental archetypes to remedy nihilism.

Because nihilism has this attitude interwoven with futility; "all thing end. nobody asked to be here. conditions are unjust. what's the point." But I think that's only partly true... and partly true is conditionally true... and truth cannot be conditional. Much like true love cannot be conditional... you must love someone equally when conditions are miserable, as when conditions are favorable, becuase that is what defines truth, and that miust be the most priceless thing for a being like the human, who constantly wrestles with the machine and the biology, the intellect and the instict, the outside world and the inside world. I believe this is one of the fundamental archetypes, modes of being, that our ancestors developed to survive and evolve in conditons not favorable to life, e.g., the Ice Age! TO me, this discovery has been a remedy to (non-clinical) depression, anxiety, hopelessness, futility, confusion, overwhelm, panic, philosphical, cognitive, physical, spiritual exhaustion. All of it can be summarized by "Something worth living for gives all your days reason to justify the struggle in becoming and being."

I think it may be.... perhaps not "the" fundamental human response to the conditions of life... but I absolutely believe it is one of the fundamental archetypes of being. It's something like an interwoven integration between...

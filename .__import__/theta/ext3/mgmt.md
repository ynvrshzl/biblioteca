## 2.0 - General Management
what's even more fascinating than the complexity of code, is how the same principles of complexity emerge in other fields, such as a general management. managing a project, managing files, managing time, there are base invariants in the systems, that provide evidence of universal laws, and a glimpse at potential solutions for solving some of the hardest problems we face today. 

these are fundamentally information based problems. it is fascinating, because we live in an age of endless information. we have explored extensively, that endless information , is by default unusable, exhausting, and potentially harmful. this is not a theory of, everything is information, rather, it is a proposition of dealing with problems at the information level. 

not only what things are, ontologically, but how we process them, phenomenologically, and are limits, epistematologically. we can see a cohesive intersection of three core areas... ontology, phenomenology, and epistemology. perhaps these are the foundations which form make cohesive General problem solving field.

breaking down these fields in depth, ontologically, the way we manage things, is essentially all about continuity. the systems that emerge, the combinatorial relationships, all of this exists ontologically, to solve the problem of continuous sequencing, essentially it's the episodic story map in information space. 

what's fascinating epistematologically, is our fundamental human limitations of what we can understand and express. Michael Polanyi expressed this in a quote "we know more that we can tell." essentially, this is tacit information; things that become encoded, compressed, grouped, punked, linked. this creates a benefit and a deficit because it allows us to essentially compress indefinite amounts of information time space, however, we can no longer directly access the source. because that requires breaking the tacit ontology; explaining things. on linking. organizing. 

what's fascinating is that, code, Project management, time management, and general forms of abstract management, all exhibit this problem; how do we balance the delicate scale between abstraction and verbosity? between explanation and integration? 

this is essentially the main highlight of this entire study, it's what I learned on this day. the problems that we face, within domains of bias, are interfaces of Information Systems laws. this means, phenomenologically, that although code seems impossibly difficult, or we seem to be drowning in information these days, even information follows a physical structure, and we can apply those engineering principles, at the information strata.

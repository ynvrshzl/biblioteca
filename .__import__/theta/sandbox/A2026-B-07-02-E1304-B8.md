title: "Doing the right thing is hard"
---

Why is doing the right thing almost always more difficult than doing what is easy? Why is it that easy decisions, behavior or paths in human life, almost always lead towards destruction? What are our limits in this range?

As much as I would like to follow perfect protocol, every single time, for every single decision, for every single event in my life, we must acknowledge that nothing in life is essentially perfect [^1]

**The Hammer of Futility**, is a double-paradox object of phenomenon. In essence, it is a conceptual object to illustrate the problem of futility... becuase simply doing what is good, does not guarantee positive consequence. As a second-strike, or "aftershock" phenomenon, being good sometimes results in pain. This is almost, paradoxical, and it ... cynical?

Argued from an existential perspective, this reaches it's most difficult problem, it is almost always easier to avoid suffering and to have never existed, than to face the ocasionally unbearable conditions of life. So why would a person work hard to be good?

That is at the central of our ethical, philosophical work. It is the heaviest matter in the Youniverse...

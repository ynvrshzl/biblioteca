In relation to Education Theory (and Information Science, Epistemology, Phenomenology, and Cybernetics...) The Chalkboard and Professor is a fascinatingly simple, yet effective enviornment for Knowledge Communication (That is what Education is in some fundamental Utilitarian sense)

There must be a reason why the Format of most of the Prestigious Academic Spaces, are still so strongly rooted in the Classical Form: Chalkboard, Professor, Daily Segmented Lessons, Homework.

It has something to do with the "Progression" element of the Material... becuase we have access to far more "effective" study enviornments, attributed almost entirely to the, almost Invasive explosion of Technology in everyday Human Life.

Yet these mediums lack a significant Dimension which is, most likely, impossible to emulate by virtual means: The Effectiveness of Bare-minimum.... almost Invariant In some sense. That must be what a Good Professor is doing... even before the Tools, Mediums and Externalization Strategies... the Education is actually in the Cybernetic Lifecycle between the Person who knows, and the person whom is Learning.

There are many fascinating implications in that relationship; Information Density, Gaps in Understanding (Implicit vs. Explicit Information), Velocity of Information Transfer, Rate of Decay of Focus, Enthusiasm & Engagement (important), Participation, Tangible Tactile Feedback... among other Metaphorical (Not Pseudoscientific, however) Attributes.

However, the most important, I hypothesize, is the Progression. Becuase, based on personal Phenomenology; that is the missing Element from Hyperspeed Information Travel (Technology) it is ironically Constraint; Gauging these propositional Attributes between Knowledge and Person.

Infact, the problem is most likely at the level of Conceptualization of Relata: Assuming Knowledge is a Material Quality in the world, and the Human Brain is simply a Sponge for Processing.

The Textbook proceeds the Professor; it is an Invariant medium of Knowledge (We have studied the book and it's Historical Formats: it is a fundamental Analog of Knowledge Communication: A precursor to Technology.)

While the Homework aspect is Questionable (Considering it's roots as somewhat of an intellectual "Punishment" for misbehaved students... though that is another discussion alltogether) the Form of Chalkboard, Professor, Open-space Presentation, (Sometimes Partial Student Participation)... this is fascinating... to think that Universities from MIT to Harvard, still adapt Education to this Deeply Traditional format... there must be something of significance

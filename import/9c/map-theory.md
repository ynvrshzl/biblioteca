Proposition: If information were to be arranged as a map (cartesian space, with x-y co-ordinates on a map/matrix. describing the information), it presents an under-explored sleuth of added dimensionality to information organization; which inherits the affordances of maps. e.g.,

Scalar dimensionality: information can now occupy clear hierarchical boundaries and categories, which are dependent on the current scale of the map. This reflects information density: i.e., large areas are proportionate to larger densitieies of information.

This is much closer to how we conceptualize and navigate the world (to some degree) as compared to classical computer one-dimensional information organization systems, which treats data, information, knowledge and categories as points, scattered across a one-dimensional space. This is most clearly observed in file-trees; which remain the standard structure of organization in computational work.

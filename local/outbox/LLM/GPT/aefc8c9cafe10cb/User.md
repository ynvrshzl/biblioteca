If "Literary devices" and even Classifications of linguistics, appear present throughout writing discussions, wikis and articles throughout the web, why is there no Academic established formal study of Authoring itself? Something like a general school of writing? A subject as integral as Mathematics, English and Science. 

I envision an "Author's Framework" or comprehensive study of the author's language arsenal, and how writing, especially stories, cybernetically teach us to think, and express, feeding back into the writing and progressing the process. This however, is much simpler when written as an abstraction, than implemented in the real-world. The reality of authoring today is much more distraction-laden and fragmented, than a cohesive study of the art of writing. This is infact, a subject that requires disciplined analysis and definition, in order to be implementable, especially in an age where writing is increasingly disturbed by the micro-decision-noise of the 21st century.

We may begin with something like "Here are components of writing, this is a sentence, this is how lexemes form into phrases, and here are different kinds of phrases. Here are perspectives, and here are linguistic devices to convey character. Here are different voices to use on stage, and here is the history of the craft of storytelling. Literary devices provide a b c, etc." It's almost like telling a story about how stories unfold. That may be precisely why such a subject has not yet been implemented; it is operating at the far outlands of the meta-sphere of analysis!

## Heurithmetics

Legend (conceptual):

- "x" any variable representing a recognized chunk of information.
    
- "x/a/b/c" hierarchical chunking of items
    
- "x...y" tweening or interpolation between spaces
    

Unused conceptually useful:

- "a:y" cross-referencing link
    
- "x/y/z:a/b" cross-reference link (complex topology)
    
- "a:b:c:d" topological square
    

- "x.y.z..." static coordinates for an information chunk
    
- "x(y, z, w)" vector coordinates in information space
    
- "a->b" arctangent
    

So this measures the continuity of events, at top-most level:

```
a,b,c,d,e,f...
```

This is essentially an "abstract schematic" of the day, or of phenomenological information management. In this case "a" represents the morning, or the first segement of a person's day. the transition from "a" to "b" is of particular significance; as transition stages are noteably some of the most taxing class of events on the nervous system. this can be represented as "a...b" where we do not essentially "tween" between points of a person's day.  

"a/a" and "b/a" can describe heirarchical aspects of a person's day, such as "a: morning, a/a: chores, a/b: exercising, etc." This is no different from building a tasklist; only the language emphasizes the topology or the orthography of information; capable of expressing a diverse class of phenomenology such as a daily schedule, a project, a complex subject, or even a psychoatlas.

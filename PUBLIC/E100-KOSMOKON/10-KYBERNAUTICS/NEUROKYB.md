I work with interwoven complex systems. I love complexity and seeing things come to life and solve real problems. But it's also exhausting when systems feel monolithic or problems feel like they're impossible shapes.

In this case, what is the hardest part of working, focusing, and managing complex information?


## Backtracing
Make one step - backtrace 5 steps - forget the first step - finally progress again - repeat.

This may be a necessary part of learning; learning what doesn't work. Learning waht isn't true. Learning what isn't useful. But not discarding this information; saving it for when it may be useful.

Backtracing (+aliases) is the phenomenon of working memory to recall non-local (not necessarily global) positions out of order, whilst maintaining the current local position. 

## C2: Ontological Objects

From what i've learned the most important ontological object is "the map of continuity." Which is essentially an working-memory map of continuity, e.g., "Why does task 'x' matter? Essentially it's the big picture, the map, the story of; why a step, a byte of action, or a decision matters in a larger frame.

The second oobj (ontological-object) is a physical externalized medium surface area.

The third oobj is an interface. A place where multiple streams of information, communication .

The fourth oobj is the most challenging: the enviornment. Fluidity between these oobj's is ...what keeps us from getting stuck...

# Principia Economica: Continuity, Equillibrium and Managing life in complex information spaces

Let's begin with research: What kind of balance is required to stay upright on a bike?

Is it something like "equillibria: gyroscopic-momentum; a constant baseline of accelleration of motion with a local field of gravity" ...or is that too mythically-alchemical of a description? This is fascinating becuase it suggests balance is not "a or b" but rather a constant momentum; a continous feedback of recallibration of movement between scales. Somewhat Cybernetic since equillibrium across a plane, requires weight distribution between "a" and "b, and compensation appears to require feedback; recallibration of "a" by the proportional weight of "b" in accordance to a balanced equillibrium; e.g., 50%

So let's say, ontologically, an equillibrium is a scale with several variables and components;

"a" and "b" as polar scales, "f" as the fulcrum, "g" as gravity", "w" as a metric of general weight (mass and gravity), "e" as the ideal equillibrium, and "f(x) = ..." as a measurement of differential compensation "c" or weight distribution "d"

## Mathematics as a language of description

This is fascinating becuase we are using mathematics to build physics to build an analytical model of a physical object. If this is accurate whatsoever; it may be useful as applied to general human life; becuase I suspect equilibrium is by definition is one of the most important (or) stable objectives of systems and life. Moreover; equillibrium requires both; feedback (compensation, distribution, etc.) and movement (momentum, feedback, callibration, analysis)

## The hidden variable; distance

Even more interestingly, the classical "equillibrium scale" contains a hidden metric; distance. Distance between "a" and "b" based on the "f" fulcrum, demands ever-increasing fine callibration of distribution, whereas an expansive distance between "a", "b" and "f" provides a slower callibration time. I think this is somewhat related to the earlier idea of gyroscopic equillibrium while riding a bicycle; although the analogy refers to constant momentum and motion, rather than a fine-tuning of stationary compensation. The ideas are orthogonal; balance requires distance and relativity-to-source as a base variable. The last part, relativity-to-source, is perhaps where resolution comes in;

Distance is perhaps one of the most fascinating, and important units of physiological well-being. Without distance; we lose sight of the big macro scale of things. The macro is where orientation and context exists; or in other words; pragmatics. The micro is where local decisions, action and implementation exists; semantics. The meta is where observation, reflection and feedback exists; analysis. There are 3 spheres of ontology here; Meta, Macro and Micro. Distance between these spheres appears to be phenomenologically and psychologically necessary. Interestingly, these spheres form a triangular structure, which can be geometrically conceptualized as a hierarchy; and hirearchies are power structures of distribution; top-bottom, bottom-up, horizontal, etc. Though this may be too alchemical to analyze further, phenomenologically.

## Transformation

Transformations are the basis of language and computation... and language is a highly-powerful, under-explored tool for human communication of ideas. This becomes tremendously relevant in today's age of extreme cognitive saturation and stimulation; with thousands of micro-decisions, graphics, visual-cognitive-noise and abnormal densities of information in a confined space. This is why I propose a reversion to classical origins of human cognition; the language. As it naturally produces two necessary enviornmental variables, necessary for human focus:

- Minimal enviornment; physical substrate for cognitive externalization. Almost no distractions between the substrate (the language and medium) and the person (psyche, intellect, cognition, expression.)
    
- Exclusionary reduction: The limited, uni-dimensional, slow nature of language requires a complete reduction of noise that is not essential, such that only the language, medium and the person are spotlight boundaries in the human resorvoir of finite resources (attention, focus, creativity, problem-solving, etc.) This does not essentially reduce everything to "the world is just me, the paper, the language, and my ideas" but it does establish an enviornment of zero-tolerance for noise. Essentially, what-you-see-is-what-you-get. With computers and high-noise enviornments today, what-you-see-is-either programmed by you, or programmed by someone else, with entirely different objectives.
    

That's one of the primary phenomenological observatoins, driving these studies.

"We construct worlds. This power is not arbitrary."

When I was at my most clinically depressed, I can almost describe the phenomenological suffocation as something like "a cry for another life... another world, but a cry for all that is beautiful and lovely and good and true." I don't believe it is naive to hope for beauty, moreover; let it be naive. We are not as God, knowing all things, and capable of all things. The human psyche is immensely complex, fragile, and paradoxically resillient.

The main idea is explored throughout our kosmology stories, within the youniverse, the duoverse, and beyond. That there is something like "different worlds" or "different lives" and that these lives or worlds can be imagined, or interpreted... and that those visions and dreams can become the driving force of our steps forward... not knowing what lies after each step, but believing in something worth dying for, and worth living for. However, that is not merely what saved my life; it was the frieghtening confrontation with death. Not by a physical encounter (though I did go through the death of a pet in my arms, which shaped, and changed me.)... an encounter with the very nature of death; the ending of all things, and how we may respond.

Moreover, I found a sustainable source of fuel, whilst uncovering the work of Dr. Jordan B. Peterson. Oddly so, his work unified religion, myth with the apparent distant psychology and history of human nature.

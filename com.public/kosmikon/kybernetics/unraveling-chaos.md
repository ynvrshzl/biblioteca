### Section 1, Class 1: Ancestral human
Yet our ancestors survived. **How so?** All paths converge to a single idea: tribe. Having more eyes to keep watch. Each member of a tribe could keep watch, and in some sense, our general scope of sensory awareness, stretched as far as our tribesmen. However, this is precisely where communication becomes the backbone of the tribe; almost literally. Our collective, shared nervous system relays information and processes enormous amounts of unfeasible computation (organically speaking).

That is a beuatiful vein of history which deserves extensive research, however, the main idea in this study is even more nuanced; enviornments are not the same as work.

As soon as our ancestors developed strategies, emerged language and fortified community structures, we had an indespensible surplus of time at our disposal; time that could be spent working on pressing issues (...these issues can get quite depressing, so we won't unpack the details of such issues here.)

This meant that instead of navigate complexity and problem-solving in nonfinite physical enviornments; we began shifting work to a significantly smaller surface area; whilst progressively expanding our range of depth. What this means is; we no longer spent days hunting the rainforests for food; we developed strategies to mitigate redundant time expidenture. The surface area became concentrated on architecture, writing, drawing, marksmanship, archery, sculpting and the miracle of modern society... may perhaps be traceable to this single episode, considering the vast epochs of human history.

Eventually, we became ever-increasingly concretized on a specific set of issues, disciplines or general "areas" of colletive human development. Eventually, discplines, subjects, fields and industries arose from this shared nervous system, so to speak.

Here is the point we are at today however; drawing back from the original argument; enviornmental complexity is not the same as disciplinary complexity. They share almost the same set of qualities and properties, as we have studied extensively with Kybernetics... and yet, the key difference is not the structural qualities of information; but the mode of access. What this implies is manifold, but at the heart of the study; we are no longer navigating physical spaces, with physical anatomy; we are navigating information spaces through cognitive anatomy. Human intellect-based work _can_ be significantly more sensitive than physical labor (citations vary, appearing to be individual-based).

## The reality of brain-fog

Brain-fog, as I experience it, is not simply the inability to focus; it is the inability to make sense of time, space and my very own proprioception.

If physical labor is prone to collapse; brain-fog is psychological collapse with forced endurance and performance. That is a recipe for burnout, and burnout, is a particularly uncharted aspect of human well-being. My personal phenomenological experiences with burnout led me to classify it as one of the most dangerous states for a human being; becuase burnout is primarily a loss of resource; not a loss of direction. Pair this loss of resource, with burnout side-effects, such as depression, anhedonia, exhaustion, procrastination and brain-fog, and those essential elements produce perhaps the antithesis of productive well-being; loss of resource, loss of direction and loss of meaning. The last elementary property, arises from something like "semantic satiation" but as applied to an individual's life in general. This metaphor fits the phenomenological experience effectively, as semantic-satiation is a temporary loss of meaning, resulting from repitious perception, of a finite surface area. This could be:

- Repeating the same word until it loses comprehensible meaning
    
- Changing colors until they lose emotional significance
    
- Staring at the same sign until it resembles shapes instead of meaning

## Enviornmental influenc

If physical enviornments are non-neutral, the same applies for virtual cyberspaces. This is studied extensively in other fields I am developing. In essence; the computer is no longer a document editor for "the office." It is a multi-modal virtual space with access with networking, coding, websites, social medias, technologies, research, knowledge, editors, applications, graphics, sound, music, games, calculation, and non-exhaustive, indefinite endless arrangements of non-finite, non-linear spaces.

The latter is somewhat avoidable, the former is not; the computer is largely responsible for brain-fog.

## Applications
The model of spheres is useful in that it unifies our understanding of how human cognition works; when analysis is detrimental, and when creativity needs to be trusted.

As a kosmonaut, I find it challenging to stop my brain from mapping the world around me. I try to understand specific parts of it, not the entirety. Even so; further fractions and fractals become  increasingly interwoven and complex, as I follow the story of how things work. 

This is necessary; "how" things work only reveals a slice of the whole. In some sense, the spheres study the resolution of our abilities; from the meta, to the iota.

From the outermost resolution, to the smallest precisions of calculation. The model does not merely "explain how math works at different levels of analysis" but "why math works, and why it must work at different levels of analysis."

Henceforth; distance defines these modes.

## Phenomenological origin
I realized this model unified many loose theories I have been tinkering with, when I realized; we can't fully understand everything. Neither ourselves, nor the world, nor the systems we build, nor our own thinking. This naturally renders logic insufficient in series; logic is merely one level of analysis, within the larger sphere of analysis.

The limits of human knowledge is known philosophically as "Epistemology," however, moving away from intellectual pondering; what role does "understanding" play in everyday human life? This infact, is the proposed axiom of the field.

### The "Micro"
Code is an exceptional example of continuity and unifaction between logic and the creativity; the code tells you nothing about how it will affect the real-world, or even it's transformed output. It merely describes a procedural series of instructions for a computer to evalute and execute; in the programmer's case; a series of assertions.

However; if one remains purely analytical; assertion of fact is merely logic. That is fun as a weekend project; but designing code that genuinely produces helpful functionality out of the clockwork of a machine, is no trivial task. Infact, we do not really even understand how to conceptualize computation formally, after computer-science.

This reveals a fascinating aspect of code, and of the micro-sphere: "the analysis only makes sense, insofar as it can be ocntinously callibrated and evaluated by a larger objective or system outside of itself."

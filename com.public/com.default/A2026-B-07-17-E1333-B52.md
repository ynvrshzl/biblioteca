# Optimization theory

the significance of optimization, intersects with phenomena such as perfectionism. 

speaking of perfectionism, this study begins with a local experience; with a simple question, why can't we just do the right things? why can't we be perfect? why can't we at least avoid mistakes, suffering, or the painful mysteries and moments of human life? 

I believe, this study provides several perspectives to deal with this fundamental ache. 

one of which, I believe for living systems, it is better to do something tiny or insignificant, and sometimes it is better to do something poorly than to not do it at all. roughly speaking, there are exceptions; sometimes we must do things as best as we can, sometimes it must be perfect. very rarely however, do we face completely binary choices.. where we are forced to choose either between perfection or nothing. but living systems operate differently, and I believe it is important to distinguish between animal and machine, because we human beings are neither animal nor machine, and we don't seem to be somewhere in between, I believe our abilities for metacognition, expresses a more significant structure... (...)


what's fascinating about optimization, as a fundamental process of adaptive system dynamics, is that optimization requires search balance. the boundaries of search base is initially generated procedurally, through means of sampling, exploring, or otherwise. what is critical during this stage, or phase, is that a system must not be overwhelmed, so as to not expand boundaries Beyond capacity. this is because optimization requires constraint. 

when we search for patterns, it is a result of constraint. when there is little to no patterns, when there are little to no constraints, I believe this is where the system becomes overwhelmed, system resources become depleted and exhausted, faster than procedural generation or regulation... what this means, is that brain fog, burnout, and exhaustion, are "maelstrom" dynamics, a downward spiral. progressively worsening, unless the environment is changed. what's fascinating is that, the environment itself is not merely physical, the psychological environment is just as real as the physical environment. 

in other words, constraint/patterns allow us to explore the current boundaries of a system, rather than exploring potential, we explore purpose; "instead of what is possible, what should I do?" if complexity / sophistication ontologically answer "this is what is possible", then perhaps telos, or something else can answer "this is what should be."

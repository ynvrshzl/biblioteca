Even before formal historical inquiry, from First-Principles; I suspect Mathematics was initially developed as a Problem-Solving language; an externalization of Human-Cognition. 

Eventually, we realized enough of our Cognition could be externalized, such that Mathematics itself was the symbolic-space of interaction; not the real-world problems. This allowed us to manipulate abstractions, and build a cohesive language. This language would later be used as an empirical tool to verify it's own claims. 

Mathematics however, is not self-defining. No system can exist in isolation. Regardless of it's analytical potential for infinite description of reality; Mathematics requires an external system.

# H
Let us research something phenomenologically fascinating; how do people respond, or heal, from the tragic death of a loved one, when they are atheistic, or believe in no such thing as an afterlife, or mercy?

If there is no such thing as the metaphysical, according to the athiests worldview; then death truly is the end of a living thing. In which case, there is most likely no materialistic nor rationalist argument, that presents itself as sufficient sustenance when dealing with tragedy, illness, chronic pain, malevolence, or in this case, death.

This is a significant argument because; it is not an argument; but an irrefutable observation of the nature of life, from our finite epistemological understanding. In that case, if we are limited, even in as much as we may understand about mathematics or computers, within a single human life, perhaps it is an act of humility, before faith, to believe we simply do not know. Thus, the atheist is left in a position of terminality; and how does that exactly shape a person? That's fascinating... and also one of the most heaviest subjects in the domains of human life.

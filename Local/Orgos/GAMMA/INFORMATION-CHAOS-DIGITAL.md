"Noise and Chaos in the Age of Information."

Technology has increasingly connected us to an endless wealth of knowledge and materials. Most of what we process in a day would have taken our ancestors seasons to process. We may be chronically overwhelmed by this excess overflow of possibility; This is precisely the problem; Technology is too powerful. Much like Uranium, Radioactivity was first discovered to be a formless, odorless substance, of immense power.

The minimalist cliche "Less is More" is only partially true; both Creativity and Learning requires constrained local search-space for sustainable pacing. The problem in the modern device is not only has that pacing become disturbed by micro-noise;

This study of focused on a recurring problem that has been heavily weighing on my conscioence for yaers now: The problem of Virtual Chaos & Noise.

The story begins: "How am I supposed to work on, much less finish, these books, if I only have a few hours in the day to focus? " The second part emerges "Of those few hours I may have, almost always, my attention is siphoned by the many micro-decisions, pulling oneself off the narrow path."

From this phenomenological telling, we see an emergence; Telos, Time, Attention, Chaos (Polydirectionality, Nonfinititude, Nonlinearity, Nonstructure).

Kybernautics studies the structural, geoometric nature of information, while metronomics is a language for calculation of information, where precision is not avaalbe. Logocography is a framework of atlas of one's life, the unifiing  for... of thees ide.as

Today, a computer is no longer an office apparatus, it is not even an enviornment; it is a superhighway of information.

It is debilitating to attempt to focus when we have 50 possibilities pulling at attention.

With visual technologies, the very noise we try to reduce, ends up in unexpected places; the nature of endless visual possibility.

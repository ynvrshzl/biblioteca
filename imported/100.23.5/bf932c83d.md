## 2.0 - Phenomenology & Introduction
This is a massively complex problem... so let's start at the most abstract, simplest representation of the problem from First Principles: "How do we make information accessible?"

Some useful references from top-of-mind (in no particular order):

- How memory works: The Human brain forgets what is not accessed (This is Ambiguous, but a Generally accepted Function.)
    
- What is accessed is relevant to some (usually external) Context. This is the basis of Information/Memory resurgence and Organic "Search."
    
- Memory is Largely Associative. This implies information (and more specifically, useful knowledge and memory...) is structurally coherent as a network, web or interlinked space.
    
- However, the graph representation of information (as common in data-science and statistics) is notoriously difficult to parse; visual representations of semantic meaning (Semiotics is useful here i think...) requires more Cognitive load to process. Thus, while the structure may be something like a "Neuronal interconnected network" the form of traversal is much more "story-like" or "path-like."
    
- The linear traversal through a non-linear system is essentially how stories work. This is how books are arranged, despite their non-linear form, e.g., Physics, Mathematics, and even Stories following a non-linear order, must be compiled in a Linear order (This is more ambiguous, becuase information can be branching, which is closer to how we actually traverse our own Connectome/Neuronal Networks...)
    
- The structure appears to be something like: "Linear traversal, branching capabilities." Though this may be represented in a multitude of forms, it most closely resembles the "Hyperlink" in which a linear body of text, can be non-linearly interconnected and thus, branched to other topics through hyperlinks
    
- The actual implementation of hyperlinks (such as Zettelkasten IDs, Titles, Schemas, etc.), appears to be another micro problem in the larger problem itself. It also appears that the Hyperlink mechanics closely emulate that of the Human Neural Networks... for example Distance Hops between one topic to another, relies on it's semantic construction in linear-space. (However, this is very granular at this point... too technical for this scope of study)
    

Zooming back outward to the main topic...

- Consolidation: Most of our learning occurs during sleep (away from mediums)
    
- Fields: Fields and Subjects are independent of substrate; yet we hold them to be true and reliable. This is fascinating, and it implies Theoretical knowledge is a precursor to Form... something like "The Essence of Function." (although this is somewhat philosophical.)
    
- Information Object Types: Linguistics is evidence that Language is not arbitrary Nurture-vs-Nature or Socialized Sound; Cultures settle on a Generalized grouping of "Information Type Objects" whether Verbs, Adjectives, etc. This implies Information (as exhibited by Language) has very real "Object Types" and those Types can be Classified, Categoried, and perhaps formed into a "Superset" language which captures the Abstract essence of information types... which would radiaclly change how we both; author and read... (This is a design constraint, so it is up for another discussion.)
    
- Knowledge is recorded in language form, but langauge only one such information type. Visual information is one domain, Auditory information is another. The Computer today can program... (though this is substrate-dependent... so it is out of the scope of this study.)

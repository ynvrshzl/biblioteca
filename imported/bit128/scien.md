The science of science is a dangerous one; i often realize "wait a minute, I am over complicating the simplest of things!" One need not calculate variables or research extensively; yet it is an innate necessity for the Scientist Archetype.

I also propose that this may be how some of the most important inventions were discovered, not merely invented. While the Scientist does indeed "over-complicate" things in the short-term, often arriving at otherwise, "intuition-based conclusion", through rigorous emprically-oriented methodologies. The scientist wants to "understand" things, to know who and why they function. This is fascinating, becuase it is a trait common amongst Engineers, Programmers and brilliant individuals. While these Archetypes or Personality Traits tilt disproportionately towards Entropy and Psychologial instability; I argue that if the person is given the correct enviornment, while in the short term they may wander "aimlessly in analysis", there may come a time when that analysis is precisely needed; in the long term.

By the time a Scientist is "done" mapping a phenomena; while most people have moved on, the Scientist understands the vast interconnected complexity of a thing, how it works, and more importantly; when it does not work. This may eventually lead to what we may call; the Engineering side of Science; the application of observation and research.

All of that complexity reveals some of the underlying firmament in seemingly "simple" or "mundane" things.

What's even more fascinating is how the Scientist is drawn to certain phenomena, and not others...

We may need to research this!

There is another thread: Bottom-up, Top-down, and Orthogonal

A tangible example of this trio to illustrate the real-world theory. Mathematics is an example of bottom-up: where we begin from axioms, literally "self-evident truths requiring no further explanation." That is metaphorically alike to "bedrock: no further excavation possible or required for foundation." Mathematics describes its discipline as "building up." Science is an example of a Top-down method; where we begin by observing the natural world and understanding how the natural world functions. This is where Science records "Laws" especially in Physics; which itself is a bridge between Mathematics and Science.

In this case, Physics is an example of an Orthogonal or Horizontal; A field which Unifies the Rules of Mathematics, with the Observation of Science; essentially providing a language into the Laws governing how Physical things function; which is one of the most important Fields we know of. In some sense, because it shouldn't be possible.

To summarize this: Mathematics begins with "what exists" and the mathematician learns "why it works."

The fascinating part is that this lifecycle is... the Mathematician eventually learns the language of Math; and discovers or even invents extensions of Math!

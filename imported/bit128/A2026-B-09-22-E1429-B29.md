Language and abstraction. 

This is a fascinating module of study; each word emerges into phrases; and this is somewhat of an abstract imagination prompt. Take this sentence for example: "She peeled the oranges from the garden floor." This presents the imagination-sphere, with an image of a scene; "Oranges, Garden floor, Girl, Peeling." The story presents these not as isolated facts of the world; but as continguous ontology; things ocurring sequentially or narratively. These 'variables' have linguistic types; verbs, adverbs, conjunctions, etc. 

The more we describe something; the closer we get to it's low-level representation; but the more information-mass we increase as a result. 

Why does this matter? Abstraction protects us from losing sight of the continuity; by collapsing excessive reconstructive data as hinting; presenting only enough that a blur of an image may be interpreted.

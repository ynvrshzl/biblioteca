Let's explore two central ideas: context and constraint.

In Linguistics, context is primarily studied in pragmatics; how the surrounding macro influences the structure of a given phrase in the micro-scale. This naturally forms a constraint, specifically of proportional relata: the macro influences the micro in reference and relation to content.

This is a why a phrase can be "taken out of context" suggesting a structural quality to pragmatics and general information conveyance. i.e., "What is going on?!" Carries a semantic content towards a negative presupposition of confusion or disorder... however, when placed within a larger context, not only does the phrase have continuity and historical reference (context), but it's grammatical, meaning has shifted in relation to a larger set. This is essentially true at all concievable stages of linguistics (and undoubtedly beyond...) from the tiniest of graphemes to the most epic of epics; small parts are made whole

Why was Socrates so keen on avoiding writing?

From brief research, it appears that Socrates rejected writing, because writing was a mimicry of understanding. To externalize, preserve or record knowledge is merely one of many necessary domains in human life; towards flourishing of life.

Arguably, knowledge should become part of you, for better or worse; this is integration. The quality of knowledge, can be metaphorically deriven by that of fruit; is it rotten, merely sweet, or a replenishing source of life? Knowledge, from the tree of knowledge... perhaps was not the original sin; but pride.

If one requires recording knowledge; what good is it if it is not used? What good is it if half of my understanding exists in a library? 

Recently, after studying brief linguistics (the field is ridiculously massive), I observed a fascinating co-relation between mathematics, language, written language, information management and architecture, the structural nature (or) qualities of information, and the human ability for meta-cognition and analysis...

Analysis, information, knowledge, observation, writing; all are indefinitely endless. Essentially. There is no "built-in-limit" switch to the human capacity to analyze. This is a problem; human life is finite; information is non-finite. We are limited essentially not by space (how much or what we can work on), but by time (how much space we can fit into a finite human life).

Writing contains the incredible quality of resolution; from the outermost "meta" lens, to the most infinitesimal quantities, "the iota" or the "metron." Infact I conceptualized this to be something like "spheres of resolution," starting from the outermost, "meta" domain of inquiry, to the "macro," as a cohesive overview of a system; something approximating a map. Zooming inwards to paths or sets of parts; "the micro," where relationships are local. Zooming furthermore, onto the "iota," the smallest individual parts; a color, a word, an action.

It's fascinating becuase; none of this knowledge was derived by writing itself; but by human imagination. Walking. Away from the computer. Away from pen-and-paper. Away from any external medium. Something like "direct inquiry with the spheres of the inner micro-cosm of human knowledge and imagination..."

Knowledge did not come from writing, or even connecting ideas in writing; such connections almost always emerge at "macro" scale; away from the computer, from the medium, from substrate.

Writing is prone to information noise; an information is somewhat independent of substrate. This is where I hypothesize a very crude representation of writing as "what if the purpose of writing is to structure information that has already been processed by organic, human means?" That's not a methodology; that's a description of an ontological process.

Writing is powerful, and as Francis Bacon famously said; "Knowledge is power", but we musn't stop merely at knowledge = power; power begets use; and more importantly; the use of power requires responsible application, especially at scales. That's where the "spheres" model is useful; socially, intrapersonally, or even systematically. Atleast, it's a hypothetical model to unify these ideas.

While writing is powerful for structuring information; The power of observation, slow-organic-growth, and proper thinking; is where the real power is applied.

Infact, this approaches something like a life-long craft or art; not a study of isolated thought; either "analytical mandelbrot hell" or "hedonic-armchair-philosophy" but rather, "how may one spend finite human life properly, knowing there is so much we are limited by, and so many spaces that demand our finite faculty? How do we use this properly?" I think that was the objective of the Greeks, and moreover; what has driven large-scale human civilization. It isn't the struggle for power, in the Marxist lens; it's something like "the struggle for applying this power, without instructions, to solve the problems we face."

This is where I propose problem-solving, as a general bedrock; if knowledge does not either directly or indirectly help solve problems; it does not justify finite human attention.

Problem solving however, requires another sphere overlapping; the metasphere. The meta analysis of problem-solving is somewhat similar to that of information...(...)

Even moreso; knowledge is not meant to last forever; so we cannot merely argue "if we can explore space, but are truncated by time, we can encode time in our space; by preserving knowledge and the material" becuase most ancient civilizations in history; are largely forgotten. Information and knowledge, then, are ultimately made for action, use, either directly or indirectly. So the story becomes a continuity; knowledge and action, power and ethics, problem-solving and sustainability.
